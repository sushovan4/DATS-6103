Itemsets and Support


6 minutes

The data is a list of transactions, each a set of items. Nothing else—no quantities, no order, no outcome column.

baskets = [
    ["bread", "milk"],
    ["bread", "nappies", "beer", "eggs"],
    ["milk", "nappies", "beer", "cola"],
    ["bread", "milk", "nappies", "beer"],
    ["bread", "milk", "nappies", "cola"],
    ["bread", "milk", "beer"],
    ["nappies", "beer"],
    ["bread", "milk", "nappies", "beer"],
    ["bread", "milk", "nappies"],
    ["milk", "nappies", "beer", "cola"],
]

items = sorted({i for b in baskets for i in b})
n = len(baskets)
print(f"{n} transactions, {len(items)} distinct items: {items}")
10 transactions, 6 distinct items: ['beer', 'bread', 'cola', 'eggs', 'milk', 'nappies']

Ten baskets is a toy. The ideas do not change at ten million, but the running time does, which is the whole subject.

Support

The support of an itemset is the fraction of transactions that contain it.

\text{support}(X) = \frac{|\{t : X \subseteq t\}|}{n}

sets = [frozenset(b) for b in baskets]

def support(itemset):
    """Fraction of transactions containing every item in `itemset`."""
    itemset = frozenset(itemset)
    return sum(itemset <= t for t in sets) / n

for i in items:
    print(f"  {i:<8} {support([i]):.2f}")
  beer     0.70
  bread    0.70
  cola     0.30
  eggs     0.10
  milk     0.80
  nappies  0.80

Note itemset <= t: for Python sets, <= is the subset test. That one operator is the whole computation.

Support extends to any itemset, not just single items:

for combo in [["beer"], ["nappies"], ["beer", "nappies"],
              ["beer", "nappies", "milk"], ["beer", "eggs"]]:
    print(f"  {str(combo):<34} {support(combo):.2f}")
  ['beer']                           0.70
  ['nappies']                        0.80
  ['beer', 'nappies']                0.60
  ['beer', 'nappies', 'milk']        0.40
  ['beer', 'eggs']                   0.10

An itemset is frequent if its support is at least some threshold you choose, usually written \text{minsup}. The threshold is a judgment, not a calculation: too high and you find only the obvious, too low and you drown.

Support is not a probability of interest

Support answers how often does this combination occur, which is mostly a question about how popular the individual items are. milk appears in 80% of these baskets, so anything containing milk starts with a large advantage.

That is why support alone never identifies an interesting rule, and why Rules, Confidence and Lift exists.

Why the obvious algorithm is impossible

To find every frequent itemset, check every itemset. With d distinct items there are 2^d - 1 non-empty ones.

for d in [6, 20, 100, 1000]:
    print(f"  {d:>5} items -> {2**d - 1:.3e} possible itemsets")
      6 items -> 6.300e+01 possible itemsets
     20 items -> 1.049e+06 possible itemsets
    100 items -> 1.268e+30 possible itemsets
   1000 items -> 1.072e+301 possible itemsets

Six items gives 63, which is nothing. A hundred items—a small corner shop—gives more itemsets than there are atoms in the observable universe. A supermarket carries tens of thousands.

So brute force is not slow, it is impossible, and no amount of hardware changes that. The next chapter is the observation that makes the problem tractable anyway.

# What brute force would mean, even at d = 6.
from itertools import combinations

all_sets = [c for k in range(1, len(items) + 1) for c in combinations(items, k)]
print(f"brute force would evaluate {len(all_sets)} itemsets")
print(f"of which frequent at minsup=0.3: "
      f"{sum(support(c) >= 0.3 for c in all_sets)}")
brute force would evaluate 63 itemsets
of which frequent at minsup=0.3: 18

Eighteen of sixty-three. Most itemsets are not frequent, and that is the opening the Apriori algorithm exploits.

Summary

  • A transaction is a set of items; support is the fraction of transactions containing an itemset, computed with a subset test.
  • An itemset is frequent when its support clears a threshold you choose. The threshold is a judgment.
  • Support mostly reflects item popularity, so it cannot by itself say anything is interesting.
  • There are 2^d - 1 itemsets. At d = 100 that is 10^{30}, so brute force is impossible rather than slow.
  • Most itemsets are not frequent—18 of 63 here—which is what the next chapter exploits.
